The ledger · the same hour in totals — magnitude, not time
One register, not eight boxes. Time runs down, as it does in the fall, and every series shares the rows — so the 13:52 burst is answerable at a glance: drop swelled, accept did not move. The old cards are gone as cards; their answers live in the ledger below, which owns magnitude and never pretends to own time.
Why down. Mikroview already reads downward — the fall drops, the brink is the top edge, newest first (#363). A metrics page whose time ran left-to-right asked the operator to turn their head between the two screens that describe the same hour. Here the brink is the same line in both.
Why the cards broke. "The cards stay" was reopened when round 1 was killed. Cards fragmented the hour into boxes that each restated the time axis. The ledger keeps every answer they gave — which rule, who talked, which device — as one strip that is honestly not a time series.
Per-column scale, declared. A shared axis would flatten drop (12/min) under accept (421/min), which is what the old overlay did. Each column's width is its own hour maximum, printed under its name — floored at 12/min so a series that whispered all hour draws as the thread it was, never inflated to look busy.

Twelve types stayed silent — no episode raised in this hour. They keep their column, drawn as the hairline it truthfully is, so the wall never hides a detector or pretends a flat chart is news:
One coordinate space, two marks. A rate earns a ribbon; an episode is a discrete thing, so it earns a tick at its minute and nothing more. Because the rows are shared with the traffic register, 13:52 reads straight across — the burst and the three flags it raised are one line, not two screens.
Sixteen types cost sixteen hairlines, not sixteen charts. Round 1 folded silent types into a sentence and drew nothing. This keeps the sentence and gives silence a shape: an operator can see at a glance that twelve detectors are running and quiet, which a sentence alone cannot show.
Identity never touches colour. Sixteen types, no hue cycle: the label carries identity, the ink carries meaning only where meaning exists — episodes raised on refused traffic sit in refused red, the rest in traffic blue. The screen survives greyscale, and the Table view proves it.
Episodes are not open work. This counts episodes raised in the hour; the rail's badge counts open unexcluded flags. The two disagree whenever triage lags, and both are true — the labels say which is which rather than reconciling them silently.

Sharp, and reachable

Device pixels. Ribbons are filled paths on a whole-pixel grid; axes and rules use shape-rendering: crispEdges, so hairlines land on a device pixel instead of blurring across two. Redrawn on DPR change, never upscaled from a raster.
Keyboard. ↑↓ move the cursor a minute, ⇧ ten, Home the brink. Each move announces the minute and its figures.
Reduced motion. The brink's breath and the ribbon's arrival are the only motion; both become instant.
