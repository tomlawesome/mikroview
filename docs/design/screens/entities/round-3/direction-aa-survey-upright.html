The drawer holds the terrain, never a verb. Pulled open, the survey slides over the dimmed roll — the same vertical map, unchanged — and choosing any stake closes the drawer onto that thing's row. Nothing is possible in the drawer that is impossible without it. The slide is 200 ms; instant under reduced motion; the scrim, the handle, Esc and a left-drag all close it.
Why a drawer and not a squeezed map. A phone-width terrain would spend the whole screen to say less than one roll row. The drawer keeps the phone honest: the record full-width always, the territory one deliberate gesture away — and because the vertical map is a narrow column by design, the drawer is its natural pocket; the desktop layout and the phone layout are the same drawing, docked or undocked.
What stays true on the small screen. The stake grammar, the shared selection (a stake tapped in the drawer is the row focused in the roll), honest geography, #490's read-only grammar (a viewer's drawer simply has no naming field to open). The mobile shell itself — where the rail goes on a phone — is #486's question, drawn minimally here on purpose.
